A practical course in six short lessons

EV batteries, from cell to road

Build a working mental model of the battery beneath an electric car: what stores the energy, what limits the range, why charging slows down, and what actually makes a battery age.

See it Begin with physical intuition, not equations.
Use it Change variables and predict what happens.
Judge it Apply the model to real ownership choices.

What you’ll be able to do

By the end, you’ll be able to read an EV battery specification without hand-waving, estimate useful range and charging time, and explain which habits matter for battery life—and which worries are mostly noise.

The learning path

  1. Inside the pack

    Travel from ions in a cell to modules, cooling plates, sensors, and the battery-management system. Separate the battery’s chemistry from the structure that keeps it useful and safe.

    Interactive: assemble a battery pack

  2. Energy, power, and range

    Make kWh and kW feel different. Connect pack size, vehicle efficiency, speed, weather, and reserve energy to the distance an EV can really travel.

    Lab: range budget

  3. Why charging has a curve

    Follow energy from charger to cell, then discover why a “250 kW car” cannot hold 250 kW all the way to 100%.

    Simulator: plan a charging stop

  4. Chemistry is a set of trade-offs

    Compare LFP and nickel-rich lithium-ion cells through the things they change: cost, energy density, cold-weather behavior, cycle life, and charging guidance.

    Challenge: choose a chemistry

  5. Ageing without the folklore

    Distinguish calendar ageing from cycle ageing. See how temperature, time at high charge, depth of discharge, and fast charging combine rather than acting as simple rules.

    Experiment: age two virtual batteries

  6. Making an informed EV decision

    Bring the model together: interpret warranties and state of health, inspect a used EV, choose home-charging habits, and decide which specifications deserve your attention.

    Final case: match an EV to a driver

How this course is shaped

Each lesson starts with a concrete situation, gives you one small model to manipulate, and ends with a decision that uses it. Equations appear only where they sharpen intuition.

Starting assumption: beginner-friendly, practical, and roughly 2–3 hours total across six sessions. You can ask to make it more technical, more ownership-focused, or shorter at any point.
