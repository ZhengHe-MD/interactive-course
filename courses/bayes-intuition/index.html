Lesson 01 · Bayesian thinking

Bayes, before the formula

12 min
One idea, three ways

New evidence should move you—not erase where you started.

Bayesian thinking is the ordinary act of revising a belief. The surprise is that the same evidence can mean something very different depending on what was plausible before it arrived.

01

Start with a weather forecast

Imagine a dark cloud appears over your walk home. In July, when rain is common, you reach for an umbrella. In the middle of a dry season, the same cloud might not convince you.

The cloud is the evidence. The season is your prior: what you believed before seeing the cloud. Your conclusion after combining them is the posterior.

The useful habit: before asking “How strong is this evidence?”, ask “What did I think before it arrived?”

02

Why “90% accurate” fooled your intuition

Picture 1,000 people. At a 1% base rate, only 10 are sick. A 90%-sensitive test finds 9 of them. But a 9% false-alarm rate also flags about 89 of the 990 healthy people.

9true positives
89false positives

So 98 people hear “positive,” yet only 9 are sick. The test did not fail. Our intuition ignored how many more healthy people there were to begin with.
