Why a positive test can still mean you're probably fine

A test is 90% accurate. You test positive. It feels obvious that you're 90% likely to have the thing. That intuition is wrong — often wildly so — and Bayes' theorem is the tool that tells you by how much.

The missing ingredient is how common the thing is to begin with — the prior. When something is rare, even a good test produces mostly false alarms. Play with it below.

Quick check

If the disease became ten times more common, the chance that a positive test is real would…

Right — the test's accuracy never changed, but a higher prior means more of the positives come from genuinely sick people. Rarity is what drowns a good test in false alarms.
