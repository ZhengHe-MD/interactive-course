# Initial Teaching Presets

These three course-specific approaches are editable starting points. They do not
prescribe a course topic, page design, or fixed lesson sequence. The agent
recommends one for the learner's current direction, explains why in `COURSE.md`,
and the learner can choose another before approving the brief.

| Preset | Course use | Research basis and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guided inquiry | Begin with a question or manipulable phenomenon; invite prediction, explanation, and consolidation. | Chi and Wylie's [ICAP framework](https://education.asu.edu/sites/default/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf) distinguishes constructive and interactive engagement from passive reception. This is a design rationale, not a claim that unguided discovery is always best. |
| Worked examples | Show a complete, explained example; gradually reduce support in related attempts. | Sweller and Cooper's [original algebra study](https://onderwijs.felienne.nl/vakdidactiek/materiaal/sweller_worked_examples.pdf) compared worked examples with conventional problem solving. Its subject and learner population limit direct generalization to every course. |
| Retrieval practice | Prompt low-stakes recall and application, give feedback, and revisit later. | Roediger and Karpicke's [original study](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/) found later retention benefits from retrieval with educational prose. A Studio course still needs explanation and feedback, not incessant quizzing. |

The catalog is intentionally small. There is no evidence here for one universally
best preset; the brief records the reason for this learner and remains editable.
